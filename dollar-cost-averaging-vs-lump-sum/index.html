Dollar-Cost Averaging vs Lump Sum: Which Wins? 100 Years of Data

SAFER, NOT RICHER.

This test asks whether a lump sum already in hand should be invested all at once or eased in with equal monthly buys, using Ken French monthly US total-market returns from 1926-07 to 2026-07 (1,201 months). With uninvested DCA cash fairly earning the T-bill rate, lump-sum beat a 12-month DCA in 69.1% of rolling windows, by an average of +3.31% (median +4.03%). Spreading the buys out longer made lump-sum win more often: 65.2% at 6 months, 73.3% at 24 months. DCA's wins clustered at market tops such as 1929, 1931, 1937 and 2008, and lump-sum's worst first-year drawdown was -65.7% (1931) against a median of -7.9%. DCA works as insurance against bad timing, not as a way to earn more.
